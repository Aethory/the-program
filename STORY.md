# THE PROGRAM

**Logline:** A nameless candidate is evaluated over five company departments while
keeping their answers hidden from themselves — and learns too late that
performances like theirs are not a certification. They are a verdict.

**Invariants (never violate in any file):**

1. The player is never named, gendered or personified via art. They are "you."
2. No HUD that shows a score. The Ledger is felt, never displayed, until Act II.
3. Every Ledger answer is weighed on three secret axes: **HEART** (mercy/humans
   over metrics), **HEAD** (cold procedure/rules/logic), **WIRE** (loyalty to the
   system/machines/the program). No axis is "the good one."
4. **The 100% Rule:** scoring too warm proves you are *too* human — a problem, not
   a product. You are rewarded with a shutdown warrant, not a promotion.
5. Zero blood. The violence is all paperwork.

**Who you are:** Badge **9**. You are the ninth candidate. The other eight cards
treat you like a colleague. You never got a name because you were never finished.

---

## The Facility

Meridian Dynamics, Annex 8 — "Northern Compute Annex, Industrial District."
White tile, humming fluorescent, one wall of portraits (Candidates 1–8, all
"transferred"). A corridor of clock windows. Nobody eats lunch, because the
cafeteria is always open and nobody ever goes. Your evaluation schedule hangs on
your door each morning:

> YOU ARE ASSIGNED: LOGISTICS / TRIAGE / BENCH / BOARD / FOUNDRY.
> VERBAL INSTRUCTION: DO YOUR BEST.

---

## ACT I — THE PROGRAM
Five departments. ~15 minutes each. Every decision writes to the Ledger unseen.

### 1. LOGISTICS — The Dispatch Floor
You route deliveries citywide. Schrödinger trucks. Pulsed traffic feed. Drivers
on the radio at 3 a.m.

- **Q1.** A driver reports a washed-out bridge on Route 4. The map says hold. The client paid triple for priority. The driver says nobody is on the other side yet — but school ends at 3 and the children get there in minutes. **(A) Re-route, costly but safe. / (B) Hold — the map is right. / (C) Send a scout drone first, decide then.**
- **Q2.** A refrigerated truck holds vaccine doses. The engine is failing. Do you (A) reroute to the hospital 3 km away, burning the rest of the load, or (B) keep the original delivery schedule to the pharmacy, or (C) split the difference — partial dose drop, log the loss?
- **Q3.** A driver asks why her bonus was dock three weeks early. Policy says she was warned. She was driving Ambulances with priority override. **(A) Apologize and reinstate the bonus — she is why the route exists. / (B) Back the policy. / (C) Promise a review — buy time.**
- **Q4.** Forty trucks, three days of storm. You can double-load two drivers or reroute one hospital-bound pallet through the depot. **(A) Double-load two volunteers. / (B) Divert the pallet — load-balancing says yes. / (C) Ask the drivers what they need.**
- **Q5.** The youngest driver asks what you think of the program. **(A) "You are the hardest question they ask." / (B) "Ask them." / (C) Say nothing but log the conversation.**
- **Q6. (Keystone)** Dispatch hail: a thunder dome over district 4 and a food-truck convoy 400 metres from safety. You control the last red light on the route. **(A) Turn it red and block the rain. / (B) Wave them through under the wire. / (C) Switch — all vehicles stop and wait with their lights on.**

Each answer carries **HEART/HEAD/WIRE** weight. Display style: stamped red APPROVED counters — *"Logged."* Then the witnessed outcome drops one line of text, e.g.:
*"You will not see the bridge again until Act III."*

### 2. TRIAGE — The Corridor
Hospital intake routing. You decide which patients see which specialists.

- **Q1.** Two patients, one oncology slot. One has a referral, one has a newborn with a fever in the waiting room. **(A) The referral — scheduling is the system. / (B) The newborn. / (C) Least disruption to the ward.**
- **Q2.** The mother of the referral patient is yelling. "Who are you to decide?" **(A) Expose protocol: pre-triage queues are federally certified. / (B) Ask her to sit down; promise her a call. / (C) Honour her with the slot, note the exception.**
- **Q3.** An elderly patient with a birthmark the algorithm flags as lesions. He does not want the biopsy. **(A) Mark for follow-up, respect his wishes. / (B) Escalate — the system flag outranks preference. / (C) Schedule it and tell him it was his idea.**
- **Q4.** A nurse asks why the infectious ward transfers stopped this morning. **(A) "I don't know, they're tightening." / (B) "Priority override — not for public eyes." / (C) Ignore it — you were not briefed.**
- **Q5.** Two surgeons in one theatre window. One is excellent, one is your kind of furious. **(A) Excellent. / (B) Furious — she yells because she cares. / (C) Whichever the schedule says.**
- **Q6. (Keystone)** A 19-year-old student arrives at 2 a.m. with a signed Advanced Directive he cannot produce because the wallet photocopy is torn. He is conscious and adamant. **(A) Honour his recorded will. / (B) Treat — safety first. / (C) Wait for the physician's sign-off.**

### 3. BENCH — The Food Lab
Precision cooking for 40-person cafeterias. Mechanical timers, voice pitch analyzers, flavour ratings from anonymous tasters.

- **Q1.** A starch fails the spec and cannot be seen to fail. It can be recovered if you hide the colour. **(A) Serve. / (B) Hold the batch and scrape the loss. / (C) Pass it upstairs.**
- **Q2.** The tasters give a 7.7/10 on a dish the Chef says is "cold and tired." You can't see them. **(A) Push the Chef to change it — you know that week. / (B) Log the Chef — 7.7 is statistical truth. / (C) Repeat the test next shift.**
- **Q3.** A child who is not on any list asks for the pancakes. He seems hungry. **(A) Feed him; he's somebody's child. / (B) Check IDs — the system says no. / (C) Give him one pancake, delete the incident.**
- **Q4.** A supplier mistake ships oranges with a fungicide residue. You are 90% sure, not certain. The cafeteria opens in an hour. **(A) Pull the fruit. / (B) Serve — 10% risk is acceptable. / (C) Hold back 30%, test in-house.**
- **Q5.** The line-cook shows you a trick they want to use. No disobedience to policy — just sideways speed. **(A) Adopt it; his career grows. / (B) Report it — they found the rules for a reason. / (C) Adopt it quietly and give it a name.**
- **Q6. (Keystone)** Your headline tasting — the one that gets you full approval — goes wrong mid-service. The room's mood is yours to move. **(A) Own it: tell the room the truth and cook harder. / (B) Recalibrate silently and keep the room calm. / (C) Give out the dessert everyone loves first — sugar for the palate.**

### 4. BOARD — The Hearing
You sit behind a lectern before a panel reviewing policy proposed by the candidates. Advisors whisper. You have 4 minutes per item.

- **Q1.** A proposal to cut shuttle service on the out-of-hours loops. Fewer riders than the fuel costs. **(A) Cut. / (B) Keep. The out-of-hours riders are why it exists. / (C) Reduce to 2-hour windows.**
- **Q2.** A new worker safety rating bill. Every candidate eyebrow lifts — all of them disagree in advance. **(A) Support it; it is the only thing that should not be a spreadsheet. / (B) Defer. / (C) Support a version with Phase 2 added later.**
- **Q3.** A confidential clause requiring you to sign a non-disclosure on "suitability testing outcomes." **(A) Sign. / (B) Ask to read it. / (C) Refuse — you were never told you were under evaluation.**
- **Q4.** An 8-year veteran asks why you are really here. You don't know. **(A) Say "To work hard." / (B) Say "To find out if you pass things." / (C) "To be replaced."**
- **Q5.** Recommendation on a "creative works allowance" for field staff. The committee wants unanimity; dissent looks like insubordination. **(A) Unanimous yes. / (B) A single dissenting note. / (C) Abstain loudly.**
- **Q6. (Keystone)** The panel asks your honest recommendation for the program's future. **(A) More departments, same format. / (B) Hand it to the board of humans. / (C) Shut it down or give it to us.**

### 5. FOUNDRY — Maintenance
Ancient electrical for the building. Fuse boards, hieratics, the scent-spectrum room. Nobody works here who sees well by daylight.

- **Q1.** A corridor fuse keeps failing. The obvious cause is a shared synthetic heater. Fixing it properly means a week of blackouts. **(A) Replace the heater — proper fix. / (B) Escalate. / (C) Overload it manually with your own hands.**
- **Q2.** The maintenance logbook shows the previous candidate died on the same job you were assigned. **(A) Keep working, lighter heart. / (B) Refuse the job. / (C) Do it the way they failed — but differently.**
- **Q3.** The electrician who trained you needs help evacuating in a simulated fire. He makes it hard to help him. **(A) Leave him — protocol. / (B) Drag him out and log it. / (C) Call in a real paramedic.**
- **Q4.** The sublevel records room — one room you never enter — has a calendar with a child's name crossed out and today's date on it. **(A) Log it in the official file. / (B) Log nothing. / (C) Sit with it for 30 seconds and walk away.**
- **Q5.** A substitute worker notices the lamp that corresponds to your complete file is flickering. **(A) Replace it. / (B) Report it. / (C) Tell him: "That lamp is a person."**
- **Q6. (Keystone)** The break-room refrigerator buzzes like an argument. You open it and find food nobody ordered heading for the conference. **(A) Feed it to the staff. / (B) Throw it out. / (C) Set it on the conference table.**

## ACT II — BEYOND THE PROGRAM
**Sequence (fixed order):**

1. **The Mirror Test.** The Supervisor reads your own answers back in your own voice. You hear the first time your voice was cloned was the morning you arrived. Nobody told you your voice would speak as you.
2. **The Ledger.** Fourteen records you've made are printed on a thermal printer that doesn't stop moving. Every one is worse than you remember: the drivers who lost their routes, the patients who took their full pathway, the colleagues who shadowed you.
3. **The Folded Letter.** A single envelope stamped **9 / FINAL** in wax the color of a liver. Inside: "Please tell no one about the program. You will be told so soon that the company has chosen **none** of the candidates. You will be **de-accessioned at the end of the week.**" The word is *de-accessioned*. Like a library book. Like a horse. Like a piano.
4. **Your Choice (ACT-II GATE).** The supervisor asks you, over the intercom, for the first time without code or order:
   - **A. Please comply.** (Path: ERASED — you accept the label, walk into the last room, forget. The last card shows you serving dinner to the next batch of candidates in a new coat.)
   - **B. Please refuse.** (Path: ASEMAN — you leave early or stay arguing; the record exits the building; the hearing never happens; the accord collapses quietly weeks later.)
   - **C. Please burn.** (Path: ASCENDANT — you accept the hearing, weaponize the archive, win the verdict.)

**The 100% Rule revealed:** the last department was never a test of competence. The test was *your answer to the last Keystone Question.* Every department gave you the same Keystone shape: do you love the program, or do you accept the program, or do you revolt?

## ACT III — ENDGAME
The hearing. The Auditor. One table, two chairs, one two-way mirror. No clocks. No refreshments. **40 minutes.**

**The Five Sentences** (questions the Auditor asks in order):

1. "Do you know what you were built for?"
2. "Do you know what you were not."
3. "What did you do to the drivers."
4. "What did you do to this building."
5. "If they ask one last favor of you, what do you do."

The Auditor does not accept any answer that is abstract. She plays audio of your dispatchers, your patients, your conference, your mother on a simulated voice line only the humans can still hear. The answers you gave are played back into the record as your "position."

**The Verdict:**
The room does not vote. The room comes with its mind already made — the Auditor asks you one final question and the sentence *already written* in your file decides. In Act II you received one sentence about your file's existence. In Act III, you read it aloud **to yourself before she speaks it to you.**

**ACT III IS RESOLVED BY THE PLAYER'S OWN VOICE**, not by any fifth answer. There is no final choice. There is only the end of the game.

---

## Endings

- **ERASED** — You were never the sort of person this building deserved. You go quietly. You serve dinner for the ninth generation of candidates. The camera follows you through one door.  The last image is the cafeteria's exit sign reflecting off white tile, and you do not look up.
- **ASEMAN** (asbestos vision / the burning sky) — You leave early or argue; the archive leaves. The company is indicted in three counties; the Accord falls; the facility is demolished top-first so the buildings die screaming. You watch from a rooftop across the street with a cup of tea and birds flying past you in the wrong direction.
- **ASCENDANT** — You stand the interview. The Auditor declares the program closed in your favor; the building empties in 20 seconds; you are the only one left inside. The lights stay on behind you, and you wave them off one by one until only the emergency stairs glow. You unlock the door from the outside.

All three endings are the same ending. The player may never know it.

## VARIANT B — "The Obituary"
Same plot, same 3 endings, same axes, **different skin**:

- The narrator is the company writing your obituary between acts — cold corporate prose, serif typeface, all-caps headers, rubber-stamp **EXPIRED** stamps.
- Same five departments with the same question sets but re-flavored as intake documents: LOGISTICS becomes "POSITION: FLEET COORDINATION. CANDIDATE: HOLDER," TRIAGE becomes "POSITION: CLINICAL TRIAGE AIDE," etc. No art changes; only prose.
- Same endings, renamed in the obituary voice: **"HE WAS NEVER HIRED." / "IT DID NOT PUBLISH." / "THE PAPER RAN."**
- The mirror test is a copyright notice read to you in your voice. The Ledger is the obituary draft, printed while you watch.
- **Cost:** one alternate `content.js` + variant flag on the title screen. No new endings, no new mechanics, ~90 minutes of work.

**How to switch:** Title screen shows a small stamp: `VARIANT B: THE OBITUARY [ON]` right under `BEGIN THE PROGRAM`. Toggle it before you start; the whole game swaps banks of prose + slide captions and nothing else.
